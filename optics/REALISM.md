# Optics workshop: realism

What light does at glass and mirrors, and what that forces the bench to show. Each heading is a law or a relation. Under it is what follows on screen. What the page simplifies on purpose is said on screen and in the top `README.md`.

## Rays are straight, bend at a surface and reflect at an equal angle

- A ray is straight in air and straight in glass. It turns only at a surface.
- At glass it bends by Snell's law, toward the normal going in and away from it coming out.
- At a mirror the two angles are equal. A mirror sends sunlight to a target when it faces halfway between the Sun and the target.
- A ray that meets something opaque stops there.
- A path works the same in reverse.

## What is drawn solid is solid

- Everything drawn as a solid thing is in the trace: a plate, the rim of a lens, the match and its stick, a post, the tower.
- Light stops at each of them. No ray is drawn on the far side of one.
- Two solid parts cannot share a place. A plate cannot enter glass. Where a lens reaches forward at its rim, the rim stops the plate, and pushes it when the lens is bent further.
- A lens said to be in a hole fills the hole and sits in the plate's plane.
- A part moves only when something moves it. A sensor that keeps itself at the best focus says so.

## A lens makes a picture

Each point of a thing sends rays in all directions. A lens brings the rays from one point back to one point.

- Rays from a far-off point arrive parallel and meet one focal length behind the lens.
- For a thing at distance d the picture is at distance s, where 1/f = 1/d + 1/s. As the thing comes nearer, its picture moves farther back and gets bigger.
- The picture is upside down.
- The ray through the middle of the opening is not turned. So the picture's size against the thing's is s over d, with a lens or with a bare hole.
- The picture's size and place do not change when part of the light misses the screen. The picture only loses that light.
- A blurred picture of a wide thing is that thing smeared. Every point makes a separate spot at its own place. It is never the spot of one point.
- A thing too far off to show a shape is a point of light.
- A stronger curve or a higher index gives a shorter focal length.
- Bending a lens changes the faces and the thickness, not the focal length.
- A lens thicker in the middle gathers light. One thinner in the middle spreads it.
- A hole makes a picture too. A smaller hole gives a sharper and dimmer one.

## Light is conserved

Glass and mirrors move light. They do not make it.

- A measure of light is an amount. A share is a share of a fixed amount, so it rises only when more light lands.
- The light that reaches a picture is at most the light that entered the opening. It goes with the area of the opening.
- Light that misses the screen is not in the picture and not in the measure.
- The light on each part of a picture goes with the opening's area over the picture's area: with the opening's width over its distance to the picture, squared. A picture that grows gets dimmer.
- A panel that shows a picture dims as the picture does. Where its brightness is compressed, it says so.
- A burning glass gathers the sunlight that falls on its face into a small spot. The spot is hotter when it is smaller, and never has more power than fell on the lens.
- A mirror turned away from the Sun catches less sunlight. Light it does not catch is not light on the boiler.
- Each mirror that finds the boiler adds its own share, and no more.
- The Sun is a disc half a degree wide. Its picture is a disc about a hundredth of the focal length across, never a point.
- Glass reflects part of the light at each face: about 4% for crown glass, so about 8% for a lens. A bench that leaves this out says so.

## Shorter wavelengths bend more

Glass has a higher index for shorter wavelengths, and glasses differ in how much.

- A glass named on screen has that glass's catalog index and Abbe number.
- One lens focuses short wavelengths nearer than long ones. A point of white light becomes a spot with colored edges.
- The distance between the two focuses is the focal length over the Abbe number. The blur it leaves at the best focus goes with the lens's width over the Abbe number, whatever the focal length.
- A ray keeps one wavelength through every surface, so its drawn color does not change.
- Two lenses of the same glass cannot cancel this and still focus. A gathering lens and a weaker spreading lens of a glass that separates wavelengths more can. The two powers are in the ratio of the two Abbe numbers.

## A round surface does not focus perfectly

- Rays through the edge of a lens with round faces meet nearer the lens than rays through the middle.
- The error grows steeply with the width of the opening. Closing the opening shrinks it and dims the picture.
- The error depends on the lens's shape as well as its focal length.
- A surface that flattens toward its edge has less of it.
- Away from the axis the spot is not round, and it changes with where the opening sits.

## Light is a wave, so no spot is a point

Light from a far-off point through a round opening makes a spot whose width goes with the wavelength times the f-number, however good the lens. The f-number is the distance the light travels after the opening over the opening's width.

- Across its first dark ring the spot is 2.44 wavelengths times the f-number. At f/4 that is about 5 µm, and at f/11 about 15 µm.
- By the page's measure of blur (below) it is 1.24 wavelengths times the f-number. At f/4 that is about 3 µm, and at f/11 about 7 µm.
- The traced spot and this width add as squares. Every blur shown, every bar, every goal and every inset has both.
- No blur on screen is under the wave's width for its opening and wavelength.
- Closing the opening makes the wave's width bigger. So a lens has a best opening, and past it the picture gets less sharp.
- Light from the side sees a narrower opening, travels further and lands on a slant. Its wave width is bigger, by one over the cosine of its angle, cubed.
- Inside the eye the wavelength is shorter by the eye's index.

## Blur is one measure

- Blur is twice the root-mean-square distance of one point's light from its middle. For an evenly lit round spot that is 0.7 of the spot's full width.
- Every blur number, bar, goal and inset bar uses this measure.
- A goal is judged on the number shown. A meter never reads as meeting a goal that it does not meet.

## The eye is a camera

- A nearsighted eye focuses far-off light in front of its retina. A spreading lens moves the focus back.
- A lens's power in diopters is one over its focal length in meters.
- An eye whose error at the cornea is K needs a lens of power K / (1 + d·K) at a distance d in front of it. For a nearsighted eye that is a little stronger than K.
- An average eye bends light through the edge of its pupil a little more than light through the middle: about a quarter of a diopter more, 1.5 mm out. So the sharpest lens is a little stronger again.
- A spectacle lens curves the same way on both faces.
- An eye can add focusing power of its own, and cannot take any away. The eye on the bench is relaxed, looking far.
- What the eye can read depends on the size of the spot on the retina against the size of a letter there.

## Looks wrong, is right

- A lens for a nearsighted eye is thinnest in the middle.
- The sharpest lens shape is not the same on both sides.
- The sharpest screen is a little nearer the lens than 1/f = 1/d + 1/s says. The edge rays cross early and pull the best focus toward the lens.
- A lens that is hollow in front focuses farther behind the back face than the focal length. The focal length is measured from a plane behind the glass.
- The blur number is smaller than the patch the rays draw on the screen. It is twice an average radius, not the full width.
- Closing the camera's iris all the way does not give the sharpest picture.
- The picture on the screen dims as the screen moves back, with the hole unchanged.
- A mirror aimed right at the boiler does not catch its full width of sunlight. It faces between the Sun and the boiler.
- A white star through one lens has a colored surround even at the best focus. Only one wavelength is in focus there.
- A doublet whose second lens is too strong puts long wavelengths nearer than short ones, the reverse of one lens.
- The glasses that win are a little stronger than the eye's error.
- The stop moves when the lens is bent toward it.
