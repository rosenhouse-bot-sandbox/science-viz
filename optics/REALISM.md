# Optics workshop: realism

What light does at glass and mirrors, and what that forces the bench to show. Each heading is a law or a relation. Under it is what follows on screen. What the page simplifies on purpose is said on screen and in the top `README.md`.

## Rays are straight, bend at a surface and reflect at an equal angle

- A ray is straight in air and straight in glass. It turns only at a surface.
- At glass it bends by Snell's law, toward the normal going in and away from it coming out.
- At a mirror the two angles are equal. A mirror sends sunlight to a target when it faces halfway between the Sun and the target.
- A ray that meets something opaque stops there.
- A path works the same in reverse.

## A lens makes a picture

Each point of a thing sends rays in all directions. A lens brings the rays from one point back to one point.

- Rays from a far-off point arrive parallel and meet one focal length behind the lens.
- As the thing comes nearer, its picture moves farther back and gets bigger.
- The picture is upside down.
- A stronger curve or a higher index gives a shorter focal length.
- A lens thicker in the middle gathers light. One thinner in the middle spreads it.
- A hole makes a picture too. A smaller hole gives a sharper and dimmer one.

## Light is conserved

Glass and mirrors move light. They do not make it.

- The light that reaches a picture is at most the light that entered the opening. Brightness goes with the area of the opening.
- A burning glass gathers the sunlight that falls on its face into a small spot. The spot is hotter when it is smaller, and never has more power than fell on the lens.
- Each mirror that finds the boiler adds its own share, and no more.
- The Sun is a disc half a degree wide. Its picture is a disc about a hundredth of the focal length across, never a point.

## Shorter wavelengths bend more

Glass has a higher index for shorter wavelengths, and glasses differ in how much.

- One lens focuses short wavelengths nearer than long ones. A point of white light becomes a spot with colored edges.
- A ray keeps its wavelength through every surface, so its drawn color does not change.
- Two lenses of the same glass cannot cancel this and still focus. A gathering lens and a weaker spreading lens of a glass that separates wavelengths more can.

## A round surface does not focus perfectly

- Rays through the edge of a lens with round faces meet nearer the lens than rays through the middle.
- The error grows steeply with the width of the opening. Closing the opening sharpens the picture and dims it.
- The error depends on the lens's shape as well as its focal length.
- Away from the axis the spot is not round, and it changes with where the opening sits.

## Light is a wave, so no spot is a point

Light from a far-off point through a round opening makes a spot about 2.4 wavelengths times the f-number across, however good the lens.

- At f/4 that is about 5 µm. At f/11 it is about 15 µm.
- A traced spot smaller than this is not the picture's real size.
- Closing the opening makes this spot bigger. Past some opening a real lens gets less sharp, not more.

## The eye is a camera

- A nearsighted eye focuses far-off light in front of its retina. A spreading lens moves the focus back.
- A lens's power in diopters is one over its focal length in meters.
- What the eye can read depends on the size of the spot on the retina against the size of a letter there.

## Looks wrong, is right

- A lens for a nearsighted eye is thinnest in the middle.
- The sharpest lens shape is not the same on both sides.
