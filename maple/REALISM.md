# Japanese maple: realism

What sunlight, pigments and a leaf do through a year, and what that forces the picture to show. Each heading is a law or a relation. Under it is what follows on screen. What the page simplifies on purpose is said on screen and in the top `README.md`.

## A leaf's color is the light its pigments do not absorb

Each pigment absorbs its own band of wavelengths. The more pigment the light passes, the less of that band is left.

- Chlorophyll absorbs near 440 and 675 nm. What is left is mostly light we see as green.
- Carotenoids absorb from 425 to 480 nm. What is left we see as yellow and orange.
- Anthocyanin absorbs near 530 nm. What is left we see as red.
- The leaf's color is worked out from the pigment amounts. It is the same leaf at every scale, so the color at Leaf is the color its cells and molecules give on that date.
- A dot of light keeps its wavelength, so its drawn color never changes. A dot that is absorbed is gone.
- Most light is absorbed near the top of the leaf, and less at each layer below. In a green leaf, light near 550 nm gets deepest.
- A pigment does not give back the light it absorbed. Chlorophyll gives back a little near 685 nm. The others give back none.

## Each pigment has its place

Chlorophyll and carotenoids dissolve in fat and sit in the chloroplasts' membranes. Anthocyanin dissolves in water and sits in the vacuole.

- Green and yellow are only in chloroplasts. Red is only in vacuoles.
- A chloroplast never turns red, and a vacuole never turns green.

## Autumn takes pigment apart and makes new pigment

Chlorophyll is broken down over days. The carotenoids were there all along. Anthocyanin is newly made, where there is sunlight and sugar.

- Yellow appears as green goes. Nothing yellow is added.
- Red appears where the Sun reaches: the outside of the crown first, the upper face of a leaf, the cells under it.
- Green lasts longest beside the veins.
- Amounts change smoothly and in step with the panel's bars. No pigment jumps.
- As chlorophyll goes, the membrane stacks come apart and the chloroplasts shrink.

## The Sun's place follows the date

At a fixed hour the Sun's height and direction depend on the date and the latitude.

- Late on a summer afternoon the Sun is high and north of west. In autumn at the same hour it is low and south of west.
- Shadows fall away from the Sun. A shadow's length is the height of the thing divided by the tangent of the Sun's height.
- A low Sun gives dimmer and warmer light, because its light crosses more air. Summer is brighter than autumn at every scale.
- Light enters a leaf through its upper face.
- The light that arrives is the same light at every scale. When the tree is dim, fewer dots of light fall in its cells.
- Once the Sun is down only the sky's light is left, a few thousandths of full sunlight. Almost no dots fall then.
- A shadow is cast by what is there. A bare crown casts a thin shadow.

## Matter is conserved

- A leaf grows from a bud over weeks. It does not appear at full size.
- A leaf falls down, lands and stays. The leaves on the ground are the leaves the tree has lost.
- No leaf hangs in the air unattached, and none passes through the ground or a branch.

## One leaf, one moment, at every scale

Zooming looks closer at the same thing at the same time.

- The date does not change while the zoom changes, and the zoom does not change while the date runs.
- What is seen at two scales is seen at every scale between them, at its true size: the cut, the cells, the dots of light.
- A cell is in its tissue, a chloroplast in its cell, a molecule in its membrane. Each keeps its place as the camera moves in.
- A cut is a flat cut through the whole leaf. What is behind the cut face is the inside of that leaf, in the right order: clear skin, tall cells packed with chloroplasts, loose cells with air between, lower skin with pores.
- Real surfaces have detail at every scale: veins in three sizes on a blade, proteins in a membrane. A flat, even surface is a sign that something is missing.

## Sizes

- The blade is paper thin, about a seventh of a millimeter, and several centimeters across.
- A chloroplast is about 5 µm across. A vacuole fills most of its cell.
- A membrane is about 4 nm thick. A chlorophyll's ring is about 1 nm across.
- Light's wavelength is hundreds of times the size of a pigment molecule. A dot of light smaller than a molecule is a picture and says so.

## Looks wrong, is right

- The leaf turns yellow though nothing yellow was made.
- Dots of green light go deeper into a green leaf than red or blue ones.
- At the scale of molecules, anthocyanin is sparse: a few molecules in the view.
