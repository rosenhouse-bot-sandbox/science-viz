# Japanese maple: realism

What sunlight, pigments and a leaf do through a year, and what that forces the picture to show. Each heading is a law or a relation. Under it is what follows on screen. What the page simplifies on purpose is said on screen and in the top `README.md`.

## A leaf's color is the light its pigments do not absorb

Each pigment absorbs its own band of wavelengths. The more pigment the light passes, the less of that band is left.

- Chlorophyll absorbs near 440 and 675 nm. What is left is mostly light we see as green.
- Carotenoids absorb from 425 to 480 nm. What is left we see as yellow and orange.
- Anthocyanin absorbs near 530 nm. What is left we see as red.
- The leaf's color is worked out from the pigment amounts. It is the same leaf at every scale, so the color at Leaf is the color its cells and molecules give on that date.
- A dot of light keeps its wavelength, so the color it is drawn in never changes. It keeps that color over any background: what lies behind it does not tint it, and it has no white core. A dot that is absorbed is gone.
- Whether a pigment takes in a dot depends on the pigments there when the dot reaches them, not when it set out. When the date changes, a dot keeps an old fate for at most the time it takes to cross one layer. No dot we see as green comes out under a red leaf.
- Most light is absorbed near the top of the leaf, and less at each layer below. In a green leaf, light near 550 nm gets deepest.
- A pigment does not give back the light it absorbed. Chlorophyll gives back a little near 685 nm. The others give back none.

## Each pigment has its place

Chlorophyll and carotenoids dissolve in fat and sit in the chloroplasts' membranes. Anthocyanin dissolves in water and sits in the vacuole.

- Green and yellow are only in chloroplasts. Red is only in vacuoles.
- A chloroplast never turns red, and a vacuole never turns green.

## Autumn takes pigment apart and makes new pigment

Chlorophyll is broken down over days. The carotenoids were there all along. Anthocyanin is newly made, where there is sunlight and sugar.

- Yellow appears as green goes. Nothing yellow is added.
- Red appears where the Sun reaches: the outside of the crown first, the upper face of a leaf, the cells under it.
- Green lasts longest beside the veins.
- Amounts change smoothly and in step with the panel's bars. No pigment jumps.
- As chlorophyll goes, the membrane stacks come apart and the chloroplasts shrink.

## The calendar follows the place it names

The page names Kyoto, so its dates follow the measured normals for *Acer palmatum* there (Japan Meteorological Agency, 1991 to 2020): the tree seen as a whole is mostly red on 5 December, and 80% of the leaves are down on 18 December.

- Leaves stop renewing chlorophyll in the second half of November, sunlit ones first. The crown is about 90% turned in the first week of December and 80% bare about 18 December.
- The order of events is kept: chlorophyll goes, anthocyanin is made, the stalk comes away, the leaf falls. The stalk does not begin to come away before the leaf begins to turn.

## The Sun's place follows the date

At a fixed clock hour the Sun's height and direction depend on the date, the latitude, the longitude within the time zone, and the equation of time.

- Every day is shown at 4 PM Japan Standard Time at Kyoto (135.75° E). That is the latest whole hour at which the Sun is still up on the day of the leaf fall: about 7.5° on 18 December. A later hour would put the whole red phase after sunset.
- Day length runs from sunrise to sunset as an almanac gives them: the top of the Sun's disc at the horizon, with the bending of light by the air. That adds about ten minutes to the day.

- Late on a summer afternoon the Sun is high and north of west. In autumn at the same hour it is low and south of west.
- Shadows fall away from the Sun. A shadow's length is the height of the thing divided by the tangent of the Sun's height.
- A low Sun gives dimmer and warmer light, because its light crosses more air. Summer is brighter than autumn at every scale.
- Light enters a leaf through its upper face.
- The light that arrives is the same light at every scale. It is the direct beam, weakened by the air it crosses, plus the sky's light. When the tree is dim, fewer dots of light fall in its cells, in proportion. At 4 PM in December a level leaf gets about a seventh of the light it gets in June.
- Once the Sun is down only the sky's light is left, under 1% of full sunlight. Almost no dots fall then.
- After sunset the light comes from the whole sky. There are no lit and shaded sides, no glints and no shadows.
- A shadow is cast by what is there. A bare crown casts a thin shadow, and the trunk and every limb cast one too.
- A shadow takes away only the direct sunlight. The sky still lights it, so a low Sun gives paler shadows.
- Clear air dims little over a park: about 1% of the contrast over 60 m with 20 km visibility. The lawn keeps its color out to the hills.

## Matter is conserved

- A leaf grows from a bud over weeks. It does not appear at full size.
- A leaf falls down, lands and stays. The leaves on the ground are the leaves the tree has lost.
- No leaf hangs in the air unattached, and none passes through the ground or a branch. Each pair of leaves sits on a drawn twig.

## One leaf, one moment, at every scale

Zooming looks closer at the same thing at the same time.

- The date does not change while the zoom changes, and the zoom does not change while the date runs.
- What is seen at two scales is seen at every scale between them, at its true size: the cut, the cells, the dots of light.
- A cell is in its tissue, a chloroplast in its cell, a molecule in its membrane. Each keeps its place as the camera moves in.
- A cut is a flat cut through the whole leaf. What is behind the cut face is the inside of that leaf, in the right order: clear skin, tall cells packed with chloroplasts, loose cells with air between, lower skin with pores. Each layer keeps to its band: no loose cell enters a skin.
- The spaces between loose cells are air and hold no pigment. What shows through them is more of the same leaf, so it takes the leaf's color.
- Cells side by side in one tissue on one date are at the same stage. A closer look shows more detail, not a different state: the followed cell looks like its neighbors from outside.
- No picture is faded into or swapped for another between scales. The camera or the object moves.
- Words on screen agree with the model on every date they are shown. A caption shown from May to November does not say it is summer.
- Real surfaces have detail at every scale: veins in three sizes on a blade, proteins in a membrane. A flat, even surface is a sign that something is missing.

## Sizes

- The blade is paper thin, about a seventh of a millimeter, and several centimeters across.
- A chloroplast is about 5 µm across.
- A grown leaf cell is mostly vacuole: about four fifths of it in spinach (Winter, Robinson and Heldt 1994). The vacuole presses the chloroplasts and the rest of the cell into a layer under a micrometre thick against the wall. So the chloroplasts lie in one layer along the wall, with vacuole between them, and a red cell is red almost to its wall.
- The vacuole's skin folds around each chloroplast, so no sap lies between a chloroplast and the wall of the cell. Seen from outside the cell, a chloroplast is not colored by the sap. Seen across the cell, it is.
- A vacuole's sap is water with sugars, salts and acids dissolved in it at tens of millimolar, a few molecules in every few cubic nanometers. It has no color of its own. A view of it at nanometers either shows them or says it leaves them out.
- A membrane is about 4 nm thick. A chlorophyll's ring is about 1 nm across.
- Light's wavelength is hundreds of times the size of a pigment molecule. A dot of light smaller than a molecule is a picture and says so.

## Looks wrong, is right

- The leaf turns yellow though nothing yellow was made.
- Dots of green light go deeper into a green leaf than red or blue ones.
- At the scale of molecules, anthocyanin is sparse: a few molecules in the view.
- The Sun never sets on the slider. Every day is at 4 PM, and on the shortest days the Sun is still about 7° up.
- Few dots of light fall in December. At 4 PM a level leaf gets about a seventh of the June light then.
- A leaf that falls early can still hold a little chlorophyll. The fall comes 17 to 25 days after the leaf stops renewing it.
