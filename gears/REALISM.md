# Gear fidget: realism

What meshing gears and machine tools do, and what that forces the pictures to show. Each heading is a law or a relation. Under it is what follows on screen. What the pages simplify on purpose is said on screen and in the top `README.md`.

## Meshing gears roll on each other without slipping

Where two gears touch, their teeth move at the same speed. So two meshing gears turn opposite ways, at speeds in the inverse ratio of their tooth counts.

- Equal gears turn at equal speeds, each opposite to its neighbors.
- A closed ring of meshing gears can turn only if it has an even count. Three gears round a corner of a cube jam. Four round a corner of an octahedron turn.
- Turning one gear turns all the others. No gear turns alone, and none stays still while its neighbor turns.
- A tooth of one gear sits in a gap of the other. This holds at every contact at once, all the way round every ring.
- A tooth never enters another tooth. The gap left between them is small: a gear held still lets its neighbor rock only slightly.

## Bevel gears share one point

The axes of all the gears meet at the center of the solid. Each gear's teeth lie on a cone with its point there.

- Neighboring cones touch along a line through that point. Their half-angles add up to the angle between the axes.
- Teeth get narrower and shallower toward the point.
- Each axis goes through the middle of a face, square to it.

## The parts are solid and are held together

- No part passes through another. The core is clear of every gear.
- A gear turns on its bearing. The bearing's inner ring is held by the screw, and the outer ring turns with the gear.
- A bearing's balls go round at about half the gear's speed, a little more here.
- A screw goes into a threaded hole. Every screw has its hole, and every hole is where its screw is.
- Taken apart, each part moves out along its own axis, in the order it was put on.
- Gears that are apart do not mesh, so one does not turn another.

## Machining only removes metal

A part starts as stock that is bigger than it everywhere. Metal comes off only where a cutting edge passes.

- The part at each step is the part at the step before, less what the tool swept through.
- A cut surface appears behind the tool as it moves, not all at once. Chips leave from the cut while it is being made, and at no other time.
- A hole, a groove or a flat stays on every later step.
- The finished part is the part in the drawing, size for size.

## Only a cutting edge touches the work, and only the work

- The tool's holder, the post, the chuck, its jaws, the vise and the spindle touch nothing they are not bolted or clamped to.
- A tool comes to the work and leaves it through open space. Between passes it backs out, returns and steps in.
- The work is held by a surface that is not being cut. A cut stops short of the jaws.
- What was held is cut in a second setup, with the part turned round and held by a finished surface.
- A tool fits where it goes. A boring bar is thinner than the hole it bores.

## One thing spins

On a lathe the work spins and the tool is fed slowly. On a mill the tool spins and the work is fed.

- A lathe tool's tip is at the height of the work's axis. A drill in a lathe is on that axis.
- The spindle turns the way that brings metal onto the cutting edge, not onto its back.
- The tool and the metal set how fast the edge may go through the metal. So a small drill spins fast and a wide cutter slowly.
- Gear teeth are cut one gap at a time. The blank is turned by one tooth's share of a circle between gaps and is held still during a cut.

## A cut is as deep as the machine, the tool and the part can stand

Small machines and small brass parts take light cuts.

- Roughing passes are fractions of a millimeter deep. A finishing pass is a few hundredths.
- A deep feature is reached in several passes. A deep hole is drilled in pecks, with the drill drawn back to clear chips.
- Roughing comes before finishing. The part is cut from the bar last.

## Surfaces show how they were made and how they have been kept

How sharp a reflection is depends on how smooth the surface is.

- Freshly cut brass is bright. Bar stock is duller.
- A turned surface has fine rings round its axis. A fly-cut one has arcs.
- A machine is painted cast iron and steel that has been used. Its reflections are broad and dull. Nothing on it is a mirror.

## Looks wrong, is right

- A cube carries gears on only four faces. Six would jam.
- The balls in a bearing go round slower than the gear.
