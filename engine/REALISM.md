# Engine: realism

What a four-stroke engine does, and what that forces the picture to show. Each heading is a law or a relation. Under it is what follows on screen. What the page simplifies on purpose is said on screen and in the top `README.md`.

## The crank, rods and pistons are rigid and linked

A rod keeps its length. One end goes round with the crank pin and the other stays on the cylinder's axis. So the crank angle alone fixes the piston's height.

- The piston is fastest about 75° from the top, not at mid-turn. It turns round faster at the top than at the bottom.
- Pistons 1 and 4 move together. Pistons 2 and 3 are half a turn from them.
- No rod stretches, bends or passes through a cylinder wall or a piston.
- The crank turns one way at a steady speed.

## One cycle takes two turns

Each cylinder does intake, compression, power and exhaust in two turns of the crank. The camshafts are driven from the crank at half its speed.

- A cam turns once while the crank turns twice. Its sprocket is twice the size, and the belt does not slip.
- A valve is down only while its lobe presses on it, and it follows the lobe's shape. A spring brings it back.
- Each cylinder sparks once a cycle. The cylinders fire half a turn apart, in the order 1, 3, 4, 2.
- Every cylinder shows its own stroke. The gas, the glow and the spark in one cylinder are not copies of another's.
- A valve never reaches the piston, even at the top between exhaust and intake, when both valves are a little open.

## Gas is conserved

Gas enters and leaves a cylinder only through an open valve.

- With both valves shut the amount of gas is fixed. It gets denser evenly as the piston rises and thinner as it falls.
- Gas goes through the ring-shaped gap round a valve's head. It never goes through the head, the roof, the wall or past the piston.
- The space above the piston at the top is never emptied. That burned gas stays and mixes into the next charge.
- Gas comes in down the intake pipe and leaves down the exhaust pipe. None appears or vanishes anywhere else.
- Every dot of gas that comes in leaves down the exhaust pipe in the end. Gas left over from one cycle is mixed through the next charge, so no gas stays in the cylinder for good.
- Mixing does not change how much gas is in any part of the cylinder. While the gas mixes it stays evenly spread.

## Gas flows from the higher pressure to the lower

Gas goes through an open valve toward the side where the pressure is lower. It goes through the ring between the valve's head and its seat, and no faster than sound.

- The gap is smaller than the piston's face, so gas in the gap moves several times faster than the piston.
- A valve that has only begun to lift lets little gas through. The pressure in the cylinder cannot fall, and a pipe cannot fill, the moment a valve starts to open. The pressure follows from the gas that is left.
- The exhaust valve opens before the bottom, with the gas still at about twice the pressure in the exhaust pipe. Gas rushes out before the piston turns. The rising piston then pushes out most of the rest.
- During intake the cylinder is a little below the intake pipe's pressure. That difference brings the air in.
- At the top, with both valves a little open, the cylinder is above the intake pipe's pressure. Burned gas goes back into the intake port, and comes in again with the fresh charge.
- The intake valve shuts after the bottom of the stroke. Until it does, the rising piston pushes some mixture back into the port.

## Pressure, temperature and density go together

In a gas, pressure is proportional to density times temperature. Sound crosses the cylinder far faster than the piston moves, so at one moment the pressure is the same everywhere in the cylinder.

- The pressure, temperature and density shown at one moment agree with each other.
- Squeezing the gas heats it. Expanding cools it, and any glow dims as it cools.
- The color and the glow of burned gas follow its temperature, the same number the panel shows. Burned gas is drawn as cooled only where it is cool: left over and mixed into the next charge.
- At one pressure, hotter gas is thinner. Burned gas is about three times thinner than the unburned gas beside it.
- At the scale of molecules, the count in view follows pressure divided by temperature. On intake the view is mostly empty. At the spark it holds about seven times as many.
- The count in view never falls below what the density gives. That holds while one gas replaces another at the spot, too.
- Hotter molecules move faster, as the square root of the temperature. At one temperature lighter molecules move faster, as one over the square root of the mass.
- The work of the gas in one cycle is the area inside the loop of pressure against volume. The power stroke gives more than the other three strokes take.

## The burn starts at the spark and spreads

The spark lights the mixture at the plug's gap. A thin flame front then moves outward through the mixture, much slower than sound.

- The spark jumps a gap between two electrodes that are part of the plug. The plug reaches the chamber through the head. Body, thread and electrodes are one piece in any cut.
- The flame starts at the plug and nowhere else. It only advances, and it stops at metal.
- The burn takes time, so the spark comes before the top of the stroke. Some mixture burns while the stroke is still compression.
- Pressure peaks a little after the top, not at the spark.
- The mass that has burned is the mass behind the front. One burn gives the pressure, the temperature, the front's place and which dots have burned. When the front has crossed the cylinder, all of the mixture has burned.
- Burned gas expands and pushes the unburned gas ahead of the front, away from the plug. When half the mass has burned, the front has swept most of the volume.
- Behind the front there is no fuel. Ahead of it the mixture is unchanged, only squeezed.
- The front moves many times farther than the piston does while the burn begins.

## Atoms are conserved

Burning rearranges atoms: 2 C₈H₁₈ + 25 O₂ → 16 CO₂ + 18 H₂O.

- Every carbon, hydrogen and oxygen atom there before the reaction is there after it.
- Nitrogen is four fifths of the air and comes through unchanged.
- About one molecule in sixty of the mixture is fuel. Any view of the mixture holds about that share.
- Fuel comes out of the injector as a spray of liquid, over a few milliseconds. Air does not turn into fuel.
- The fuel takes time to evaporate and to mix. The mixture is even only late in compression.

## One object at every scale

The page is one engine seen from nearer or farther. Zooming in only enlarges.

- What is in view at two zooms is in view at every zoom between. Nothing joins or leaves the gas with the zoom.
- A dot of one size only grows on screen as the zoom goes in.
- The spot that the Molecules scale looks at holds the gas that the dots bring there. It holds burned gas until fresh air replaces it, and fuel only once the mixing gas has brought it.
- A cut takes away what is on one side of a fixed plane. The plane does not move with the zoom, and a piece that is cut away fades where it is.
- A cut takes the gas with the metal round it. Gas in a cylinder that is drawn is drawn, in every cylinder and at every zoom.
- Moving parts are never cut.

## Words and labels agree with the picture

- A label points at the thing it names for as long as it shows. When the thing leaves the view, the label goes.
- The heading, the card and the key change together, and name the scale and the stroke that the picture shows.
- A number in a sentence is the model's number.

## One clock, one road

- When time is slowed, the crank, the valves, the gas, the spark, the wheels and the road are slowed alike.
- A tire rolls without sliding, so the road passes at the speed of the tire's rim.
- In one gear the crank turns a fixed number of times for each turn of the wheels.

## Looks wrong, is right

- Fuel-colored gas in the intake port before the spray. It is mixture pushed back before the valve shut.
- Burned gas in the intake port just after the top. It was pushed back while both valves were open, and it comes in again.
- Burned gas at Molecules while the stroke is still named compression. The burn began before the top.
- A nearly empty view at Molecules on intake. A gas is mostly empty space.
- No fuel at Molecules until late in compression. The spot is 13 mm from the plug, and the fuel reaches it as the gas mixes.
- Fewer dots in view at the Cylinder scale as the piston nears the top, while the gas gets denser. The gas is there, under the head, which hides it from that side. The Flame scale looks into that space.
- No orange in a cylinder at the Engine scale for about 60° either side of the top. The head's near edge hides the top of every cylinder from there.
- Gas over only the far half of a piston. The near half of the block is cut away, and the gas with it.
- A piston that stands out in front of the gas. Moving parts are not cut, and the gas is.
- Dots that sit still in a port while its valve is shut. Gas there has nowhere to go.
- Hot-colored gas all along the exhaust pipe. The model does not cool gas in the pipe.
- Loose atoms, and hydrogen atoms in pairs, at Molecules during the burn. The page says that each atom is drawn going straight to the molecule it joins.
- Gas that slides sideways across the view at Molecules, and between Flame and Molecules, early in intake. The fresh air the dots bring replaces the burned gas at the spot, and both move through it together.
- Dots of a new, smaller size between Flame and Molecules. Each size stands for a hundredth, then a tenth, of the gas of the size before, and takes over as the zoom goes in.
