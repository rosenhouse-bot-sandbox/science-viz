# Shoulder: realism

What bones, muscles and tendons do, and what that forces the picture to show. Each heading is a law or a relation. Under it is what follows on screen. What the page simplifies on purpose is said on screen and in the top `README.md`.

## Bones are rigid and stay in their joint

The ball of the arm bone turns in a shallow socket on the shoulder blade. In a healthy shoulder it stays within a millimeter or two of the socket's middle.

- No bone enters another. The arm stays out of the ribs and the chest.
- The ball turns in place. It does not slide out of the socket as the arm moves.
- The shoulder blade turns with the lift, about one degree for every two at the ball.
- The joint stops at its limits. A pull or a control that reaches a limit goes no farther that way.

## A muscle only pulls, along its own line

A muscle shortens between its two ends. What it does to the arm depends on which side of the ball it passes.

- A muscle pulling alone moves the arm the way that shortens that muscle. It never gets longer while it pulls.
- Over the top of the ball, a muscle lifts the arm: the supraspinatus and the middle of the deltoid.
- Behind the ball, a muscle turns the arm out: the infraspinatus and the teres minor.
- In front of the ball, a muscle turns the arm in: the subscapularis.
- The front of the deltoid brings the arm forward, and the back of it takes the arm back.
- Which side of the ball a muscle passes can change as the arm moves. So what a muscle does depends on where the arm starts.

## Length change is leverage times turn

When the arm turns through an angle, a muscle's length changes by its leverage (the distance of its line from the joint's center) times that angle.

- The length shown for a muscle and the turn shown for the arm agree by this relation.
- When one muscle moves the arm, the muscles on the other side get longer by the same relation. They are stretched, not pulling.
- A muscle with little leverage for a movement hardly changes length in it, and cannot be what makes it.
- Published leverages give the size of each muscle's effect. For turning the arm in and out, the cuff muscles have several times the deltoid's leverage.

## A muscle can shorten only so far, and keeps its volume

Only the red belly shortens, to about half its longest working length at most. A tendon stretches a few percent and no more. Muscle is mostly water, so its volume stays the same.

- A belly gets thicker as it shortens and thinner as it is stretched.
- A tendon keeps its length and width. All the change of length is in the belly.
- No pose needs a belly shorter than it can become. A muscle that would have to is slack there, and slack shows.
- A tendon stays fixed to its place on the bone, unless the condition shown is a tear.

## A pulling muscle takes the shortest path over what is under it

A muscle under tension is taut. It runs straight where nothing is in its way and lies on the bone or the muscle under it where something is.

- No muscle or tendon passes through bone or through another muscle.
- A pulling muscle has no folds or kinks. A fold means the path is longer than it need be.
- A muscle that wraps over the ball stays on the ball's surface as the arm turns. It does not lift off or cut through.
- When a bone turns about its own length, a muscle fixed to it is carried by its end. It wraps round the bone only as far as the shortest path needs.

## The layers keep their order

From the outside in: deltoid, bursa, cuff tendons, joint. The acromion is a roof over the supraspinatus tendon, with the bursa between them.

- The order never changes with movement. Nothing passes through the layer beside it.
- The bursa is a thin sac of fluid. It stays between the tendon and the acromion, lies on the tendon, and slides. It does not crumple, lift off or get pierced.
- The subscapularis lies between the shoulder blade and the ribs. It is seen from the front.
- A part that is pulling or being explained can be seen. If the opening view hides it, the view turns.

## The cuff holds the ball down while the deltoid lifts

The deltoid's pull on a hanging arm is mostly upward along the bone. The cuff muscles pull the ball into its socket, which lets the arm turn and not slide.

- With the deltoid pulling alone, the ball rides up toward the acromion.
- With the cuff torn through, the ball sits higher and the space under the acromion is narrower.
- The tendon's attachment passes under the acromion in the middle of a lift. That is where the space is narrowest and where a swollen tendon or bursa is pressed.

## Weight and load decide which muscles work

A muscle works when the load would move the joint the other way.

- A relaxed hanging arm swings as a pendulum, about once every second and a half.
- In an exercise, the muscles shown working are the ones that oppose the band or the arm's weight in that position.
- A muscle is not shown working in a movement it has no leverage for.

## Looks wrong, is right

- The same muscle moves the arm differently from a lifted start than from a hanging one.
- The muscles opposite a pulling muscle get longer, though nothing is pulling on them from outside.
