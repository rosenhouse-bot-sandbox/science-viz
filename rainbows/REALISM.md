# Rainbows: realism

What light does in rain, and what that forces the picture to show. Each heading is a law or a relation. Under it is what follows on screen. What the page simplifies on purpose is said on screen and in the top `README.md`.

## A drop sends light back in a cone

Sunlight arrives as parallel rays. A ray that enters a round drop, reflects once inside and leaves is turned by at least 138°. Many rays leave close to that angle. So a drop looks bright when it is 42° from the line that runs from the Sun through the eye.

- The bow is a circle 42° from the point opposite the Sun. The shadow of the viewer's head falls on that point.
- A bow belongs to an eye, not to a place. If the eye moves, the bow moves with it.
- The top of the bow stands 42° less the Sun's height above the horizon. With the Sun higher than 42° there is no bow over flat ground.
- A falling drop is in the bow only for a moment. At the top of the bow it is dark, then it sends red, then the rest in order to violet, then a faint white.
- After one reflection no light comes back beyond 42°. So the sky is brighter inside the bow than outside it.
- Two reflections make a second bow near 51°. It is fainter, and its order is reversed. The sky between the two bows is the darkest.
- Light goes from the Sun to the drop to the eye. No ray starts at the eye.

## Light bends by Snell's law and reflects at an equal angle

- The whole path of a ray lies in one plane through the drop's center.
- Inside a round drop the ray meets the wall at the same angle every time.
- That angle is below the critical angle, so the light is never totally reflected. Most of it leaves at each meeting. The bow is made by the small share that reflected.
- At a flat patch of surface there are three beams: arriving, reflected and passing.

## Shorter wavelengths bend more

Water's index is higher for shorter wavelengths.

- The ray we see as violet leaves at 40.6° and the one we see as red at 42.4°. Red is on the outside of the first bow, and the bow is about 2° wide.
- A ray keeps its wavelength through air and water, so its drawn color does not change along its path.
- Sunlight is a mix of wavelengths. They come apart only because each leaves the drop at its own angle.

## Light is conserved at a surface

The shares reflected and passed at a surface add up to what arrived.

- One photon in is one photon out. The counts on each path follow the shares.
- Most of the light goes straight through the drop. About a twentieth makes the first bow and about a two-hundredth the second.
- The bow's light is strongly polarized. The reflection inside is close to the angle at which one polarization does not reflect.

## Light is slower in water

Light travels at c in air and c/1.33 in water. Its frequency is the same on both sides.

- Crests are 1.33 times closer together in water, and they advance 1.33 times slower.
- Crests on the two sides of a surface meet along it without a break. That is why the ray bends.
- Dots of light inside the drop move a quarter slower than outside and sit closer together.
- Changing the wavelength changes the crest spacing in proportion.

## Why water slows light

The light's electric field pushes the electrons in each molecule. They are driven far below the frequency at which they would resonate.

- Electrons move in step with the field. The nuclei hardly move.
- Each layer of molecules sends out its own wave, a quarter cycle behind. Added to the arriving wave, it makes the total fall behind.
- Shorter wavelengths are nearer the resonance, so the electrons move farther and the light is slowed more. This is the bending above.
- Liquid water has about 33 molecules in a cubic nanometer. Air has almost none at that scale.

## The scales are one world

- The Sun is in the same direction at Orbit, Rain and Drop.
- The drop at Drop is one of the drops of Rain, in its place. The patch at Surface is a patch of that drop, and the molecules are in that patch.
- Light's wavelength is about two thousand times smaller than a raindrop and more than a thousand times larger than a molecule. A picture with a few crests across a drop, or a crest among a few molecules, is not to scale and says so.
- The air and its weather are a layer a few thousandths of the Earth's radius thick.
- Rain falls straight down in still air at a steady few meters a second. Small drops are round.
- Rain falls from cloud, and the Sun's side of the sky is clear.
- Clouds are carried with the ground under them. They do not drift over a globe that is not turning.
- If rain is slowed so that it can be followed, everything in the scene is slowed alike, or the page says what is not.

## Looks wrong, is right

- Light leaves the drop at every meeting with the wall. Nothing is trapped inside.
- The second bow's order is the reverse of the first's.
